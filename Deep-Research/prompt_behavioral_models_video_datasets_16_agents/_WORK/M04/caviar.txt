<div class="Section1">

<h1><span lang="SQ" style="">CAVIAR Test Case Scenarios<o:p></o:p></span></h1>

<p class="MsoNormal"><i><span lang="SQ" style="">Video clips
created July 11, 2003 and January 20, 2004</span></i><span lang="EN-US" style=""><o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="SQ" style="">&nbsp;</span><span lang="EN-US" style=""><o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><b><span lang="SQ" style="font-size: 14pt; font-family: Arial;">Introduction</span></b><span lang="EN-US" style=""><o:p></o:p></span></p>

<BR>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="SQ" style="">For the CAVIAR
project a number of video clips were recorded acting out the different scenarios
of interest. These include people walking alone, meeting with others, window
shopping, entering and exitting shops, fighting and passing out and last, but
not least, leaving a package in a public place.</span><span lang="EN-US" style=""><o:p></o:p></span></p>

<BR>
<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="SQ" style="">The first section
of video clips were filmed for the <a href="http://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/rbf/CAVIAR/"><i>CAVIAR project</i></a> with
a wide angle camera lens in the entrance lobby of the <a href="http://www.inria.fr/index.en.html"><i>INRIA Labs</i></a> at Grenoble,
France. The resolution is half-resolution PAL standard (384 x 288 pixels, 25
frames per second) and compressed using MPEG2. The file sizes are mostly
between 6 and 12 MB, a few up to 21 MB.</span><span lang="EN-US" style=""><o:p></o:p></span></p>


<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="SQ" style="">A typical frame
from the image sequences is below. It shows three individual boxes (yellow) and
one group box (green). There are several people in the video sequence that are
not boxed because they do not move over the course of the sequence.</span><span lang="EN-US" style=""><o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US" style=""><br>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="SQ" style="">&nbsp;</span><span lang="EN-US" style=""><o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p><span lang="EN-US" style="">The second set of data also
used a wide angle lens along and across the hallway in a shopping centre in
Lisbon. For each sequence, there are are two time synchronised videos, one with the
view across and the other along the hallway. The resolution is half-resolution
PAL standard (384 x 288 pixels, 25 frames per second) and compressed using
MPEG2. The MPEG file sizes are mostly between 6 and 12 MB, a few up to 21 MB.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="SQ" style="">All data is
publicly available and on this page they can be downloaded in MPEG2 format or
split into JPEGs. <b>If you publish results using the data, please acknowledge
the data as coming from the EC Funded CAVIAR project/IST 2001 37540, found at
      URL: <a href="http://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/rbf/CAVIAR/"><i>http://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/rbf/CAVIAR/</i></a>.
    </b>
      Usability is Creative Commons BY-SA:
<img border="0" width="101" height="35" src="by-sa.png">
  </span><span lang="EN-US" style=""><o:p></o:p></span></p>


<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="SQ" style="">&nbsp;</span><span lang="EN-US" style=""><o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p><span lang="EN-US" style="">The ground truth for these
sequences was found by hand-labeling the images, as in the example shown above.
The JAVA programs for the interactive labeller can be found </span><a href="xmlcaviarguibob.zip"><i><span lang="EN-US" style="">here</span></i></a><span lang="EN-US" style=""> (unsupported). This is the <a href="xmlcaviargui/manual/manual.pdf">userguide</A>.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p><span lang="EN-US" style="">The finite state automata
that describes the allowable roles, activities and sequence of situations in
each context is </span><a href="labelingstates.pdf"><i><span lang="EN-US" style="">here</span></i></a><span lang="EN-US" style="">.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="SQ" style="">The XML grammar
for the ground truth labeling files is <a href="http://groups.inf.ed.ac.uk/vision/DATASETS/CAVIAR/CAVIARDATA1/gt_file_format.txt"><i>here</i></a>.
Note that these XML files are large (eg. averaging 1.5MB), so viewing one of the files in a browser window can take a minute
or more to load up because of the default formatting of the XML.
The groundtruth XML is based on the CVML language (<A HREF="http://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/rbf/CAVIAR/PAPERS/listcvml.pdf">CVML- An XML-based Computer Vision Markup Language</A>), which was 
presented at IPCR.
CVML is described in more 
<a href="http://www.mindmakers.org/projects/cvml">detail</A> at
<a href="http://www.mindmakers.org/">mindmakers.org</A>.
The CVML detail includes a C++ based 
<a href="http://corelibrary.sourceforge.net">CoreLibrary</A>
which supports 
reading and writing the XML (amongst other things).
<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<P>
A <A HREF="http://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/rbf/PAPERS/pets04.pdf">discussion</A> about the INRIA datasets was presented  at PETS04. The ground truth labelling notation discussed in the paper has changed
to XML and some minor details have changed, but most of the concepts and discussion are still useful.
</P>

<P>
Some tracked targets in 17 of the sequences have had additional information 
about their head, gaze direction, hand, feet and shoulder positions added. 
(Not all because we have no more time and money at the moment.)

frames are duplicated and the expected next frame is missing. Thus, the
