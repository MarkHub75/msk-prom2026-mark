# W1_09 — Tracking continuity versus useful safety alerts

Research completed 2026-10-23; access window 03:16–03:22 UTC (06:16–06:22 Europe/Moscow, UTC+03:00). Scope: primary papers, pinned TrackEval source, four tiny evaluator examples. No industrial dataset/model benchmark, device test, or proof of LinQ execution was performed.

## Decision and evidence boundary

**W1-09-F01 [own inference from S01–S06].** Track quality and alert quality require separate evidence. HOTA/IDF1/MOTA evaluate detections and their association to annotated trajectories; they do not read PPE ownership, violation state, alert delivery, cooldown or episode semantics. Good tracking can support temporal policies but cannot certify them. A tracker ID is a sequence-local token; it does not establish an employee’s name, badge identity, legal identity, or even cross-camera identity. An event ID and a track ID should be distinct fields.

**W1-09-F02 [documentation/paper].** HOTA explicitly separates detection and association; its paper demonstrates failure modes of prior metrics, including MOTA’s emphasis on detection and IDF1’s global trajectory matching behavior. These are metric-design results on MOT tasks, not measurements of industrial alert performance. Ristani et al. explain that identity-based and event-based tracking measures answer different user questions; their “event-based” CLEAR errors are tracking decisions, not delivered safety-alert episodes. [S01, S06]

## Inputs and scope of identity truth

**W1-09-F03 [source inspection; proposed annotation contract].** The metric routines consume aligned frame lists `gt_ids`, `tracker_ids`, pairwise similarity matrices and totals of IDs/detections/timesteps. The MOTChallenge adapter reads frame, ID, x/y/width/height, GT mark/class or tracker confidence; it computes box IoU, remaps IDs to contiguous indices, and rejects duplicate IDs within a frame. Preserve stable GT person IDs across every interval where annotators can establish continuity; tracker numeric IDs need not equal GT numeric IDs. GT has to identify which person is which, independently of predictions. [S02–S05]

Proposed minimum record: `sequence_id, camera_id, frame_index, source_timestamp, gt_person_id, bbox, annotation_status, visibility, identity_link_status`. Add `episode_id, violation_type, observable_state, onset/end bounds` for alert evaluation, and `pred_track_id, state_owner_id, emitted_alert_id, delivery_timestamp, reset/transfer_reason` in predictions/logs. These extra fields are our proposal, not TrackEval inputs. Specify whether IDs persist across complete occlusion, exit/re-entry, a file boundary, camera restart or camera change. Uncertain linking must be recorded as uncertain, not guessed to obtain a score.

## Metric contracts at the pinned revision

**W1-09-F04 [source inspection, S02].** HOTA uses alpha values 0.05, 0.10, …, 0.95. It builds a whole-sequence alignment matrix from similarities, performs per-frame Hungarian assignment on alignment × similarity, and retains assigned pairs meeting alpha. At each alpha: `DetA=TP/(TP+FN+FP)`. For a matched GT/predicted-ID pair with m matches and G/P detections, association Jaccard is `m/(G+P−m)`; AssA averages this over TP detections. `HOTA_alpha=sqrt(DetA_alpha*AssA_alpha)`; summary HOTA averages alpha values. LocA is mean matched similarity, reported separately. Localization affects HOTA via alpha integration: **HOTA is not sqrt(DetA*AssA*LocA)**. `HOTA(0)` in this implementation indexes the first alpha, 0.05.

**W1-09-F05 [source inspection, S03].** Identity uses configurable similarity threshold 0.5 by default. It counts potential threshold-qualified matches per GT/predicted-ID pair over the sequence, then globally assigns trajectories one-to-one by minimizing IDFN+IDFP with dummy assignments. `IDTP=GT_detection_count−IDFN`; `IDF1=2IDTP/(2IDTP+IDFP+IDFN)`, `IDR=IDTP/(IDTP+IDFN)`, `IDP=IDTP/(IDTP+IDFP)`. These counts are detection instances across frames, not unique workers or switch counts. A good segment excluded by the best one-to-one trajectory assignment can still incur identity errors. Sequence aggregation sums ID counts and recomputes ratios.

**W1-09-F06 [source inspection, S04].** CLEAR defaults to threshold 0.5. Hungarian matching favors continuation of the previous assignment by adding 1000 before similarity, subject to the threshold. IDSW compares each matched GT ID with its last matched tracker ID, including after gaps. For positive GT count, `MOTA=1−(FN+FP+IDSW)/GT_detection_count`; it may be negative. `Frag` accumulates returns to matched status after interruptions, excluding the first matched segment; it is different from IDSW. MOTP is mean matched similarity in this adapter, not physical position accuracy. Sequence aggregation sums counts and recomputes ratios. Implementation-specific empty branches and gap behavior need care (F09).

Units: all above ratios are dimensionless; raw code returns fractions, displayed tables scale floats to percent. IDSW/Frag/FP/FN are counts, not counts per hour. HOTA combines sequences using summed detection counts and TP-weighted association terms before recomputing; it is not an unweighted mean of sequence HOTA. The base metric summarizes alpha arrays by their mean. Class-averaged and detection-averaged methods differ; pin the chosen aggregation, class scope and empty-class option. [S02–S05]

## Ignore, unknown, empty and configuration failures

**W1-09-F07 [source inspection, S05].** MOTChallenge preprocessing is a benchmark-specific pedestrian policy: match against GT at IoU 0.5 and remove predictions matched to designated distractor classes; filter GT by target class/mark; this adapter has no crowd-ignore regions. `DO_PREPROC`, benchmark selection and class mapping therefore change scores. It is not a generic rule for unknown PPE or unobservable people. An industrial adapter needs its own explicit ignore policy and tests. Merely omitting unlabeled people makes predictions against them appear false positive, and omitting hard intervals can overstate association quality.

**W1-09-F08 [source inspection; own inference].** Empty conventions do not prove correctness: identity returns zero-valued ratios with IDFN or IDFP set on an empty side. HOTA returns HOTA zero and LocA one for an empty side (including both empty). CLEAR’s sequence-empty early branches return initialized MOTA zero, rather than applying its normal formula; combining counts later can differ from interpreting that per-sequence zero as performance. Record GT/prediction counts and report no-evidence cases as such. A LocA of one with no TP is a convention, not perfect localization. Unknown violation state is not representable as an identity score; report its coverage separately. [S02–S04]

**W1-09-F09 [locally executed small examples, not a benchmark].** `W1_09_SOURCES/small_examples.py` loads the unchanged pinned metric modules and skips package initialization. Python 3.11.9, NumPy 2.4.2, SciPy 1.17.1; only runtime aliases `np.float=float`, `np.int=int` were supplied because the 2022 code uses removed aliases. No matching/counter logic was changed. The script supplies preprocessed IDs/similarity directly, so it does not validate file parsing or preprocessing. Results in `small_examples.json`:

| Synthetic input | MOTA | IDF1 | HOTA | IDSW | Frag |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 frames, 1 GT person, perfect similarity and stable ID | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| Same 10 perfect detections, ID0 for 5 then ID1 for 5 | .9 | .5 | .707107 | 1 | 0 |
| 3 GT frames; ID0 / no predictions / ID0 | .666667 | .8 | .666667 | 0 | 0 |
| Same, but middle has an unrelated IoU-zero prediction | .333333 | .666667 | .577350 | 0 | 1 |

The last comparison precisely localizes a pinned implementation failure: CLEAR lines 70–76 `continue` on an entirely empty GT/tracker frame without resetting `prev_timestep_tracker_id`; the ordinary matching path resets it at lines 102–107. Thus absence of reported Frag does not guarantee uninterrupted output. The extra unrelated prediction correctly adds an FP but also changes interruption accounting. This is a narrow reproducible observation, not a claim about every CLEAR evaluator or a patched benchmark. Preserve official scores and expose a separate gap diagnostic; document any future evaluator correction/version.

## Full-ID and no-ID routes

**W1-09-F10 [proposed protocol].** Full IDs: annotate target persons across complete evaluated clips, define occlusion and re-entry linkage rules, preserve dense negatives and visibility masks, then run the fixed adapter/config. Report HOTA/DetA/AssA/LocA, IDF1 with its counts, MOTA with FP/FN/IDSW and Frag, per-sequence counts and pooled totals. Keep online/causal output distinct from offline relabeling; retroactive smoothing may improve identity scores while arriving too late to help a live alert. Evaluate the same frozen alert policy on the produced tracks.

No full IDs: do not claim HOTA, IDF1, MOTA IDSW or whole-clip identity continuity by assigning a fresh GT ID to every frame or using tracker IDs as truth. You can evaluate exhaustive per-frame person/state recognition on annotated frames, and independently annotated violation episodes/delivered alerts under a stated matching policy. Manually linked short intervals can support a clearly named **annotated-window tracking diagnostic** only: report eligible windows/persons/duration, how selected, identity exclusions and boundary censoring; reset IDs at boundaries and do not pool them as if continuity across boundaries were known. A few inspected alerts cannot estimate missed episodes without independent review of non-alert footage.

## Alert regression cases and denominators

**W1-09-F11 [proposed tests; not existing benchmark rules].** Match alerts to independent person-linked violation episodes where identity is observable, using the event contract owned by W1_05. Do not use equality of raw tracker IDs as the correctness test. Preserve all delivered duplicate alerts before any analysis deduplication. Report explicit denominators below; rates on selected diagnostic clips describe those clips only.

| Controlled case | Failure to expose | Proposed diagnostic and denominator |
|---|---|---|
| Continuous visible violation; replace track ID mid-episode without changing boxes/state | Cooldown is keyed only to ID, so duplicate alert | Additional delivered alerts after the first eligible episode alert / matched episodes; separately episodes with a post-switch duplicate / episodes containing a switch |
| Worker A violates; IDs swap at crossing with compliant B | A’s persistent violation/evidence transfers to B | Wrong-person state/alert transfers / adjudicated crossing opportunities; count episodes and duration of incorrect owner attribution |
| A compliant, B violating; swap IDs | B inherits suppression or A’s timer, missing or premature alert | Missed eligible episodes and alert delay relative to observable onset; denominator all independently annotated eligible B episodes |
| Partial and complete occlusion, return same person with same/new ID | Unknown treated as compliant; premature reset; unbounded carried state | Re-alerts, missed alerts and wrong carryover by occlusion stratum; denominator occlusion-return cases with independently resolvable identity |
| Person exits; different person later receives recycled numeric ID | Old state suppresses or contaminates new person | Incorrect carried-state cases / ID-reuse opportunities; test reset reason and track lifetime |
| Same person resolves violation then repeats it | Cooldown hides a genuinely new episode | Detected/delivered second episodes / eligible repeated episodes; specify clearance and new-onset rule |
| Tracker reconnect/restart or file boundary during ongoing episode | Repeated alert, silent timer reset, stale state | Delivered duplicates/misses / restart-spanning eligible episodes; mark censored onset |
| Same person, stable perfect tracking, wrong PPE ownership or delayed delivery | High tracking score coexists with wrong or late alert | Person-state/ownership correctness and timely delivered episode recall with their own GT |

Proposed causal logs: frame/source timestamp, current track ID and lifetime, person-state evidence, unknown/visible flag, accumulator value, active episode, cooldown expiry, transfer/reset decision, alert creation and delivery. This makes state propagation reviewable without asserting that a track is a worker identity. Report both numbers of events and coverage; confidence intervals and exposure accounting belong with W1_06/W1_13.

**W1-09-F12 [own counterexamples].** With 1,000 perfect detections split into two long IDs, one switch costs only 1/1,000 MOTA, yet a per-ID alert engine may emit two alerts for one continuous episode. Conversely, a more fragmented tracker can still deliver one correct alert if episode state survives a proven same-person relink. Both examples are thought experiments about downstream policy, not measured model outcomes. A high HOTA with swapped PPE labels remains possible because PPE labels are absent from the tracking inputs. A high offline IDF1 can coexist with unacceptable live decision delay because trajectory matching uses the whole sequence and has no delivery clock. Therefore selecting a tracker only by one pooled score can reverse the decision relevant to operations.
