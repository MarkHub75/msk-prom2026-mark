## Decision: define the deployment claim before selecting groups

**W1-07-F01 [INFERENCE from S04/S05/S06].** A random frame split cannot support a claim about unseen cameras when train and test share those cameras. It may answer a much narrower question about sampled frames conditional on the observed installations. “New data” must specify whether it means later footage from the same camera, another camera in the same workshop, another site, or new people. None is interchangeable with another. Larger blocks are not automatically a more accurate estimate of the same quantity: they can change interpolation into extrapolation. Do not promise that a universal camera/day gap removes all dependence.

| Intended claim | Outer test grouping / chronology, proposed | Permitted development information | Residual limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Later operation on configured known cameras | Earlier development blocks, then untouched later recording sessions/days from those cameras; preserve whole episodes and input windows | Earlier footage and declared configuration for these cameras | Does not establish new-camera transfer; weather, production process and worker mix may still change |
| New camera within represented sites | Disjoint camera/installation IDs, with distinct validation cameras | Training cameras and separate validation cameras; declared target setup only | Shared site, uniforms and simultaneous events can remain dependent; claim is conditional on represented sites |
| New site/workshop | Disjoint site IDs with cameras nested within site | Development sites; separate validation sites if available | Few sites imply narrow evidence; one held-out site is a case study, not all-factory reliability |
| Adapted deployment on a new camera | Split each held-out camera into a declared setup/adaptation portion and a later scoring portion; or a preregistered causal online protocol | Only explicitly permitted setup labels / unlabeled observations | This evaluates the adaptation procedure plus model; not zero-shot transfer |
| Only one short video available | Keep source episode together; use whole material for debugging if no honest holdout exists | All visible debugging material | Demonstration/regression evidence only; no independent new-camera score |

**W1-07-F02 [PAPER, S04].** Roberts et al. connect split design to temporal/spatial/hierarchical dependence and the intended prediction task. Their guidance warns that blocking can create extrapolation and that residual autocorrelation alone can underestimate required blocking. This ecological methodology motivates the table; it supplies no industrial-video block duration, sample size or accuracy guarantee.

## What the official split APIs actually guarantee

**W1-07-F03 [DOC+CODE, S01/S02].** scikit-learn **1.6.1**, `GroupKFold(n_splits=5, shuffle=False, random_state=None)`, splits by supplied group labels. A group is held out together; across folds every group is in test once; number of groups must be at least `n_splits`. In `_split.py:610–660`, `shuffle=True` permutes unique groups then uses `array_split`; `shuffle=False` greedily assigns larger groups to the currently lightest fold by sample count. Labels `y` do not stratify this algorithm. No timestamps, original-video provenance or site relationships are inferred.

**W1-07-F04 [CODE counterexample, S02].** The 1.6.1 API prose says test sample counts are approximately balanced when shuffling. The inspected shuffled branch balances numbers of unique groups rather than their sizes. With group sizes 1000,1,1,1 and two folds, each receives two groups, hence sizes 1001 and 2. This is a hand-worked consequence of code, not an executed sklearn test. Therefore record actual fold camera counts, exposure and event counts; never rely on “balanced” as a class/exposure guarantee. `shuffle` and `random_state` were added in 1.6, so commands copied to older installations may fail.

**W1-07-F05 [DOC+CODE, S03/S02].** `TimeSeriesSplit(n_splits=5,max_train_size=None,test_size=None,gap=0)` supplies expanding-prefix training and later index blocks. `gap` is a number of **samples**, not seconds. `_split.py:1238–1300` ignores `groups`, computes test starts from array length and `train_end=test_start-gap`; it does not sort timestamps or enforce camera independence. Equal temporal spacing is needed for equal-duration test folds. A concatenated multi-camera array or dropped-frame stream therefore needs an explicit timestamp/group manifest. Neither API alone implements grouped, chronological video evaluation.

**W1-07-F06 [PAPER+official correction, S06].** WILDS/iWildCam is an applicable methodological counterexample from camera imagery, not a PPE dataset recommendation. Its official release notes say dataset v1.0 randomly split ID images, allowing the same seconds-long sequence across training/ID validation/ID test. Dataset v2.0 keeps a camera-day in one ID partition, still sharing cameras across partitions. The paper separately defines camera-disjoint OOD evaluation. **WILDS package version and iWildCam dataset version are different version axes.** A public benchmark name or random seed does not prove independence.

## Source lineage, near-duplicates and limitations of deduplication

**W1-07-F07 [PAPER, S07; INFERENCE].** Barz/Denzler distinguish exact, near-duplicate and highly similar images; modified copies can differ in color, scaling or translation. Their candidate retrieval plus manual review demonstrates why exact equality alone is insufficient. The study is image classification and does not quantify video leakage here. Its journal/arXiv abstract ambiguously describes a relative “accuracy” change while experimental tables report **classification error rates**; no numerical transfer claim is used here.

**W1-07-F08 [PROPOSED].** Establish original-video lineage before extracting clips, frames, crops or augmentations. Preserve `original_asset_id`, source acquisition ID and parent transformations. All derived views of a protected source/episode inherit its split. Renaming a clip, transcoding H.264 to another bitrate, changing fps or extracting every tenth frame creates no independent observation. A byte hash detects identical files; a decoded-frame hash detects some lossless equivalent encodings; perceptual hashes/embeddings retrieve some transformed candidates. None proves that different files are independent or that all overlap is absent. Static backgrounds can be similar across distinct events; false duplicate matches require review rather than automatic deletion.

**W1-07-F09 [PROPOSED].** Construct a dependency graph for **relations that the intended claim forbids crossing**: shared original capture, overlapping source time intervals, copied/re-encoded segments, same continuous event recorded by multiple cameras, or same site if evaluating unseen sites. Connected components are indivisible split units. Not every shared person must be forbidden: the answer depends on whether the claim requires unseen people. Unknown lineage gets `unknown`, not a fabricated unique ID. If constraints produce one giant component, the dataset cannot provide the requested independent comparison; collect another acquisition unit or narrow the claim. Do not break the component merely to obtain a desired percentage split.

**W1-07-F10 [PROPOSED].** Purge or keep together overlapping input/label windows around temporal boundaries: include the model’s past/future context, temporal smoothing, confirmation interval and complete episode boundaries. A gap larger than one frame can still leave the same violation on both sides. The required gap depends on these windows and dependence in the data, and remains a measured/declared design choice. When continuous recordings are long, blocking entire sessions/days can be more meaningful than treating every file as independent; splitting two adjacent recorder files is insufficient.

**W1-07-F11 [UNKNOWN].** Unknown foundation-model pretraining overlap cannot be certified clean by deduplicating only the team’s fine-tuning files. Record pretrained checkpoint source, hash/version, disclosed training corpora and cutoff when available; inspect disclosed memberships or URLs where possible. Say “no duplicate found by method M among available assets,” not “pretraining contamination excluded.” New privately acquired evaluation footage reduces direct known reuse risk but does not by itself establish broad target representativeness. No inspected source establishes the provenance of the model the team will actually use.

## Selection, adaptation and annotation changes are part of the evaluated procedure

**W1-07-F12 [DOC, S08; INFERENCE].** Official scikit-learn guidance keeps test observations out of fitting and model choices, including learned preprocessing. For the frozen-model route, weights, checkpoint selection, NMS/association thresholds, per-class cutoffs, calibration, temporal confirmation, cooldown and ignore/unknown rules all belong to development. A threshold changed after seeing test errors is another selected variant; the same test is no longer an untouched estimate for that selected variant. Do not relabel it independent because no gradient update occurred.

**W1-07-F13 [PAPER+CODE, S09].** Unlabeled test use is not inherently invalid: Tent is an explicit test-time adaptation setting. Inspected `Tent.forward` updates during testing, supports episodic reset, and its evaluation script resets at corruption/severity combinations. Thus a general “never fit anything at test” rule describes frozen inductive evaluation; declared adaptation answers another question. Tent on image classification does not establish useful or deployable PPE adaptation.

**W1-07-F14 [PROPOSED].** Every run declares one of: (a) frozen model and preprocessing; (b) target configuration from pixels, e.g. operator-drawn ROI/zone, with provenance and no label-driven selection; (c) supervised target-camera adaptation using a separate labeled setup subset; (d) unsupervised target adaptation; (e) transductive access to the entire scored stream. These are disclosure categories, not organizer permissions. For (d)/(e), specify exact observations available, ordering, batch size/composition, parameters/statistics updated, optimizer/steps, reset boundaries, warmup/scored intervals, prediction-before-or-after-update and whether any future frame is accessible. Chronological online claims cannot use later test frames to improve earlier predictions. Hyperparameters for adaptation are selected on development domains. A labeled-test search remains label-driven tuning regardless of category.

**W1-07-F15 [PROPOSED].** Annotation correction after error inspection needs an audit trail. Freeze predictions before adjudication; retain old GT, new GT, reasons, annotator/reviewer and timestamps. Prefer review blinded to model identity and apply the clarified rule to the whole affected evaluation subset, including unalerted intervals, rather than only “fixing false positives.” Report scores on corrected GT with explicit version. Once those observations influence model/policy design or the truth policy is tailored to a variant’s errors, use that set for development and seek fresh holdout for subsequent performance claims. Legitimate independent correction is not automatically forbidden, but it must not silently alter the denominator in favor of one model.

## Proposed split manifest and conditional routes

**W1-07-F16 [PROPOSED schema].** One row per source-time interval, with child asset tables rather than frame-only filenames:

```text
asset_id, file_sha256, original_asset_id, acquisition_session_id,
site_id, camera_installation_id, camera_view_config_version,
start_timestamp, end_timestamp, timezone, timestamp_quality,
source_start_pts, source_end_pts, decode_version,
event_family_ids, dependency_component_id, duplicate_review_status,
split_role, fold_id, split_reason, split_manifest_version,
annotation_version, class_mapping_version, coverage_status,
allowed_use, adaptation_role, annotation_access_timestamp
```

Use null/unknown with reasons for absent provenance. Camera relocation or substantial view change gets a versioned installation/view ID while retaining the physical-device relationship. Event-family IDs connect cuts of one event and synchronized multi-camera views without claiming worker identity. Keep source acquisition and download dates separate. Freeze the manifest plus content hash; log exceptions and official splits without silently repairing them.

**W1-07-F17 [PROPOSED routes].**

1. **Only supplied demo clips:** establish provenance and inspect potential same-source overlap; annotate reproducible scenarios and report behavior on these clips. If no independent group remains, stop at functional/regression evidence.
2. **Several independent acquisitions, known-camera deployment:** reserve later untouched sessions for test; use earlier sessions for training and validation, with temporal boundary handling. Freeze complete pipeline before test. No generic 80/10/10 ratio is justified by current inputs.
3. **New-camera/site transfer:** outer split at camera/site level; do tuning on remaining groups using distinct inner validation groups. A small grouped CV can aid development, but selecting the best CV variant and publishing its selected CV value as fresh test evidence introduces selection optimism. Keep an outer test if the available independent units permit it; otherwise label CV as exploratory and report group-level variability.
4. **Declared target adaptation:** choose rules on development cameras, then run the exact setup/adaptation/scoring procedure on untouched target cameras. Compare adapted and frozen systems on the same scored intervals, with adaptation cost and available target information stated.
5. **Late organizer evaluator:** preserve and reproduce its mandated split/result; run the independent group-aware analysis separately when possible. A team’s protocol does not override official rules or invent missing organizer permissions.

Rare classes may be absent in validation/test groups. Report per-class counts and undefined metrics rather than splitting one episode across partitions to make every class appear. More frames cannot create more camera/site units. Group balancing may use prespecified counts for allocation, but not repeated searches for favorable model scores.

## Exposure, aggregation and audit experiment

**W1-07-F18 [PROPOSED reporting contract].** Splitting has no true-positive matching rule itself; the GT/matcher is inherited unchanged from the detector/person/event protocol. Always report the number of independent sites, camera installations, sessions, original videos and episode families per partition, alongside evaluated camera-hours, observable GT person-time or GT episode counts as appropriate. Keep unknown/ignored/unannotated exposure separate. “Accuracy over frames” and “average over cameras” estimate differently weighted quantities. For a downstream event protocol, pooled recall is summed matched GT episodes / summed eligible GT episodes; equal-camera mean recall averages the defined per-camera recalls. A camera with no eligible GT episode has undefined recall, not zero or one; report how many such cameras exist and retain their exposure for false-alert analysis. False alerts per evaluated camera-hour uses total delivered false-alert count / total **evaluated, sufficiently annotated** camera-hours under the same delivery/cooldown rule. Do not average camera rates without declaring weights. Unit and matching definitions must be identical before comparing variants. These are proposed reporting requirements, not official competition metrics.

**W1-07-E01 [PROPOSED, not run]: lineage/split audit.** Decision: whether an “independent” result can be claimed, or whether data must be regrouped/recollected. Hypothesis: filename/frame splits cross protected lineage/event/domain units. Inputs: original manifest, timestamps, camera/site mapping, transformation records, optionally decoded candidate frames; no LinQ needed. Independent unit: outer claim’s camera/site/session plus protected dependency components. Fixed: target claim and forbidden crossing relations. Vary: original split versus component-aware split, without training or choosing on test performance. Procedure: enumerate intersecting group IDs and overlapping source intervals; retrieve duplicate candidates with a documented method/version and review them; inspect temporal windows crossing boundaries; reconcile group and exposure tables. GT: human-reviewed lineage/same-event links, not model-predicted violations. Matching: shared IDs / interval intersection plus adjudicated candidate pairs; numerator confirmed forbidden cross-split pairs or crossing components, denominator candidate pairs reviewed or components audited, explicitly **not** a universal leakage prevalence estimate. Save manifest/hash, candidate/review decisions, exclusions, counts, unresolved unknowns and split-diff. Stop after all known protected relations are checked and unresolved provenance is logged; no universal candidate-distance threshold. If violations found, freeze a corrected split before further selection; if none found, state audit coverage and method limits. Success proves split conformance for available provenance, not target-domain accuracy or unknown pretraining cleanliness.

## Open questions and handoff

- **W1-07-Q01 (organizer/data, decision-critical):** What is the intended target: configured cameras, unseen cameras, unseen sites, or a fixed hidden contest distribution? Does an official split exist? Until answered, keep claim-specific routes separate.
- **W1-07-Q02 (data):** Are camera/site/session IDs, original capture times and clip lineage available? Without them, grouping confidence is limited; candidate deduplication alone cannot repair provenance.
- **W1-07-Q03 (organizer):** Are target ROI setup, unlabeled adaptation and labeled target-camera setup permitted? Preserve disclosure fields now, do not infer permission from Tent or WILDS.
- **W1-07-Q04 (training):** Which checkpoint and training corpora/version are actually used? Unknown pretraining overlap must remain explicit.
- **W1-07-Q05 (future measurement):** What temporal windows and reset rules does the full pipeline use? This determines purging and the meaning of adaptation exposure; no fixed gap is established.

→ **datasets:** F01/F06/F08–F11/F16 require source lineage, nested camera/site IDs, whole-event grouping and explicit unknown provenance before frame extraction; E01 is the immediate audit. → **training_strategy:** F12–F17 require inner development selection, frozen outer test, full-policy threshold locking and target-adaptation declaration. → **violation_methods/video_architecture:** F10/F14/F18 require temporal context, reset/order and exposure definitions to match actual operation.
